## 0. The question and the one-paragraph answer

Raw-first makes capture cheap and universal: a document or turn is stored,
chunked, split into L0 raw turns and embedded the moment it arrives, and is
answerable from raw at once. Extraction (3 self-consistency passes + memory
context + `extraction_check` + resolver commit) is the expensive step, and is
now queued and grouped. The owner's point is that a brain does not deeply
process everything it perceives, processes at different depths, and does not
keep everything it processed equally accessible. The question is what should
decide depth and residency, without inventing thresholds.

**Answer.** Three decisions, each driven by a signal the literature has
measured and the codebase already produces:

1. **Depth is decided by a cheap, calibrated L1 pass** (one Jev decision request
   per extraction group: is there a durable claim, a state change, an
   instruction, a correction of something we hold?) plus two _free_ signals
   the stack already computes (embedding novelty against prior scenes; entity
   overlap with memory already held). This is levels-of-processing plus the
   "novelty/prediction-error gates encoding" result, and it is the design
   LightMem, Nemori and EverMemOS converged on.
2. **Promotion to deeper levels is event-driven**: a settled segment boundary
   (already the scene trigger), explicit instruction/correction (eager, for
   correctness), a retrieval that had to read raw (already built as
   relearn-from-raw), and repeated access. Retrieval-triggered processing is
   the strongest-grounded trigger we have (reconsolidation; LazyGraphRAG) and
   the one we already measured cheapest.
3. **Residency is use-driven decay over the _processed_ layer only**
   (ACT-R/Anderson–Schooler need-probability; verified use, not surfacing),
   with D0 (raw) never degraded — serving falls back to raw, so a cold or
   never-processed memory is slower and less structured, never absent.

Every stage below ships behind its own measurement; the go/no-go leg is named.

**What the evidence supports, and what it does not.**

1. _Measured, repeatedly:_ batching by boundary and deferring the update is
   the main cost lever at roughly held accuracy (LightMem, Nemori, EverMemOS,
   RecMem, LangMem debounce). Our grouped background reader is already this;
   aligning groups with segments (not a 20 s clock) is the remaining step.
2. _Measured, few systems:_ gating deep processing on prediction error
   (Nemori), recurrence (RecMem), or query time (LazyGraphRAG) cuts
   construction cost substantially. Nobody publishes gate recall — how much
   later-needed evidence the gate dropped. We should, since our batteries know
   the gold evidence.
3. _Measured once, synthetic:_ predictability of future queries decides whether
   offline pre-processing pays (Sleep-time compute).
4. _Not measured anywhere found:_ residency policies. MemoryOS's heat formula
   is the only published one and it uses hand weights; nobody reports an
   ablation of tiering vs uniform at matched token budget (the same gap
   memory-research-2026-08 found for age-graded serving).
5. _Not done by anyone surveyed:_ retroactive capture of tagged raw neighbours
   when a salient event lands (behavioral tagging).

The D1 request asks, of one group rendered exactly as the extractor would see
it (with the same `contextPrefix` — #673 showed a judge without the
extractor's context loses transitions, STEV 10→6):

- `durable` (noul): the text states something about a person, thing, plan or
  state that would still be worth knowing next week (not greetings, acks,
  logistics of this chat);
- `change` (noul): it states that something previously true is no longer true
  or has changed (a transition);
- `instruction` (noul): it gives the assistant a standing instruction or
  preference ("remember…", "always…", "from now on…");
- `correction` (noul): it corrects or retracts something said or recorded
  earlier;
- `identity` (noul): the speaker states who they are / their name / role;
- `salience` (score 0–3): the rubric from importance-scoring-design-2026-08
  (incidental / routine / notable / identity-central), reused verbatim so the
  D1 stamp and a later `knowledge_fact.salience` agree.

Plus the free signals, computed without any model call: **novelty** (1 − max
cosine of the group centroid to the user's recent scene centroids / raw
windows, the same computation as `scene-segmentation.ts`), **hot-entity
overlap** (NER mentions ∩ entities with verified use in the last N days or
with open COMPETING facts), **source prior** (`get_source_reputation` vertical;
connector channel from the source plane).

### 4.2 Triggers

**Eager triggers — correctness, never gated, never delayed by a window.**
They exist because the gate can be wrong and these errors are visible to the
user on the very next turn.

| E-1 | D1 `instruction` ≥ floor, or `identity` ≥ floor                                                  | enqueue a D2b read of that turn immediately (own dedup key, no batch window); until it commits, the instruction lane serves the raw turn verbatim as a _pending_ instruction (marked as such, cited by `episodeId`) | `instruction-lane.service.ts` reads only the extracted predicate; a skipped or delayed instruction is a behaviour bug, not a recall miss                                                                                                       |

**Ordering without invented weights.** Stage 1 uses a lexicographic queue:
eager (E) → read-before-understood (T-3) → retroactive capture (T-8) → settled segments that pass the gate
(T-1/T-2) → hot-entity (T-5) → repeated access (T-4) → idle backlog by D1
salience (T-6). Stage 7 replaces the idle ordering with a _learned_ priority:
a logistic model whose label is observable — "this document was later cited
by a supported answer, or triggered T-3" (outcome telemetry,
`memory_outcome` / tool observations) — and whose features are the D1
probabilities, novelty, hot overlap and source prior; refit by the existing
calibration refit job (`src/ai/calibration/calibration-refit.service.ts`).
The floors of T-2 are not hand-set either: they are fitted on labelled
batteries to a declared **gate recall ≥ 0.98** of evidence-bearing documents
(see §4.4) and then frozen for the held-out check.

### 4.3 Residency: hot / warm / cold for processed memory

Scope: facts, edges, beliefs, scenes, summaries. **D0 is not in scope** — the
"never degrade L0" law from memory-research-2026-08 (irreversible compression
−35pp vs raw; staleness detection needs the raw you would delete) stays; raw
leaves only through GDPR forget.

- **Activation.** Per processed item, ACT-R base-level activation over its use
  history: `B = ln(Σ_j (t_now − t_j)^−d)`, `d = 0.5` (the ACT-R default that
  Anderson & Schooler fitted to environmental need), where the events `t_j` are
  its creation plus each **verified use** (`memory_outcome_stat`, 0107) — not
  each surfacing (`fact_usage.readCount` is self-reinforcing, the 0107 review
  finding). One number, no hand weights, parameter from the literature.
- **Tiers are relative, not absolute.** Hot = the top-K by `B` per (tenant,
  user) under a capacity budget (the prompt/candidate budget is the real
  constraint); warm = the rest of the active set; cold = items below a
  quantile whose last verified use is older than the compaction horizon.
  Absolute thresholds would drift with tenant size; capacity budgets do not.
- **What each tier means for serving.** Hot: default candidate set, profile /
  briefing eligibility, full ranking weight. Warm: retrievable by search, down-
  ranked by `B` (this is the existing decay factor, re-anchored). Cold: out of
  the vector leg (embedding dropped, as compaction already does for closed
  facts), still in BM25, timeline, `get_fact`, provenance and asOf reads; its
  `source.episodeIds` point at raw, which remains fully served.
- **Exempt from decay** (they are state, not events): current values of
  `single_active` predicates of a live entity, `instruction`, identity-central
  (`salience = 3`), open COMPETING pairs. Decay applies to `append_only`
  episodic facts, edges and scenes — exactly the class `promotion-runner`
  already folds.
- **Re-heat = reconsolidation.** A cold item cited by a supported answer goes
  back to hot (a verified-use event), and if the answer was read from its raw
  turn rather than from the item, T-3 fires. Serving degrades
  facts → summaries → raw lanes → L3, all of which exist.
- **This is the value-based forgetting that is not built**, and it replaces
  the age key of `COMPACTION_HOT_RETENTION_DAYS` with a use key; the
  compaction/promotion machinery (summary stands in for its members in one
  transaction) is the mechanism to reuse.

### 4.4 Measurement

Batteries and their noise, as measured (memory notes 2026-09-18…25): memfit
32 (structural noise was 23–30 before #650/#651; now 31–32), STEV 12 (+ serve
25), dogfood (22 → 57 questions; one build scored 16–21 on 22 across rounds,
so ≥2 rounds and `SYNTHESIZE_ANSWER_CACHE=0`), the held-out PR-history battery
(generalisation check: floors and priorities are fitted on the others and
_only evaluated_ here). All eval spend on the cheap model; cost computed before
a run.

New instrumentation (stage 0) that every later stage reads:

- **cost per ingested KB, by depth and tier** — tokens × price from each
  extractor / decision / embedding call, attributed to the document(s) of the
  group; flex vs standard separately.
- **depth distribution** — share of documents at D0/D1/D2a/D2b/D2f, per tenant.
- **time-to-D2** and **raw-served share** — fraction of supported answers whose
  citations are episodes of sub-D2 documents.
- **gate recall** — for each battery question, the documents holding its gold
  evidence (the batteries know them): was any of them left at D0/D1?

### 4.5 Honest risks

- **The gate's value is bounded by how much is noise.** If most captured text
  in real tenants is evidence-bearing (a CRM record, a decision log), T-2
  skips little and the saving comes from grouping (T-1) and the flex tier,
  not from gating. Stage 3's would-skip share answers this before any gating
  ships.
- **Benchmark genre bias.** LoCoMo/LongMemEval-style golds often sit in small
  talk; a gate tuned to our dogfood may fail there. The held-out battery and
  the recall floor are the guard; the eager and T-3 paths are the backstop
  (a skipped turn that is later needed is read at that moment).
- **Latency to facts.** Segment-level extraction delays facts by the settle
  period; correctness then rests on the raw lanes and the eager lanes. Stage 2
  must land with or before stage 1.
- **Decision-plane language skew.** The Jev documentation says non-English is
  weaker; gate recall must be reported per language (RU/EN at least), and the
  floor may be per language.
