## §4 — Why the strict number is flat: the full answer (research pass)

Direct question from the owner ("почему вообще не растет"). Three
findings, each independently verified this session:

**(1) The metric is step-function-blind.** Sampled strict-judge misses
are not garbage: summarization predictions carry correct dated facts at
15-20% rubric coverage and score a binary 0. A leg that lifts rubric
coverage 15%→45% measures as zero on the strict judge — five sessions
of legs optimized against a step function. The nugget protocol (§1) is
the growth-visible metric; strict binary stays as the regression guard.

**(2) Three dead abilities have WRITE-SIDE structural causes.** The
macro is flat because event_ordering (5%), summarization (7.5%) and
temporal (25%) hold it down while preference (70%) and KU (47.5%) are
healthy. Ordering: our extraction collapses a session's mentions onto
one validFrom — order is unrecoverable from facts, and the scan lane
reconstructs it from segments only. Summarization: no progressive
digest mechanism exists; golds want the narrative ARC from the earliest
exploratory beats, which fact extraction keeps thinnest.

**(3) Competitor source study (graphiti + mem0 checkouts, full digests
in the session transcript).** mem0 OSS v3 is ADD-only — no
update/delete decisioning, no digest, no event dates, no ordering, no
graph (removed ~4000 lines); everything relevant is Platform-only and
their own published BEAM-10M numbers are weak exactly where we are
(temporal 16.3, ordering 20.2 — "open problems across the field").
Graphiti has the two mechanisms we lack, production-shaped:

- **Per-fact `reference_time`** — each extracted fact anchors to ITS
  originating episode's event time (LLM returns `episode_indices`;
  prompt renders `[Episode N] (timestamp:…)` headers), plus two
  anti-collapse extraction rules (a more-specific restatement is a NEW
  fact, never generalize). This is the exact fix for our
  mention-collapse.
- **Saga: rolling per-conversation digest** with TWO watermarks
  (ingestion-time filter watermark — monotonic, backfill-safe;
  event-time semantic watermark — advance-only), one LLM call over the
  delta, merged into bounded state with an idempotence guard ("return
  unchanged if no new durable facts"). This is the scratchpad (§4 of
  the brief) already designed.
- What graphiti does NOT have: any temporal/ordinal reranker (all ~20
  retrieval paths sort by relevance) and any duration math (they
  serialize `valid_at – invalid_at|Present` ranges and let the answer
  LLM do arithmetic). Our chronological coverage lanes are already
  ahead there.
- From mem0 worth lifting anyway: the Platform temporal write-schema
  (event-time interval + ongoing/completed status + precision +
  memory-type, used as an ADDITIVE ranking boost, never a filter), the
  entity promiscuity penalty (`sim × w × 1/(1+k(n−1)²)`), and
  query-length-adaptive BM25 sigmoid normalization.

**The V12 build agenda that follows** (ordered by expected lift):
1. Per-mention `mentionedAt` on derived facts (reference_time port:
   episode timestamp headers + episode_indices in the deriver prompt,
   anti-collapse rules) → ordering + temporal at the substrate.
2. Saga-style rolling digest as the insight-slot section
   (two-watermark incremental merge; the LIGHT scratchpad evidence +
   graphiti's production shape agree) → summarization.
3. Temporal write-schema + additive temporal boost (mem0 Platform
   design, SurrealDB datetime/duration natives do the math).
4. Nugget judge as the primary leg metric (strict = guard), abstention
   row decisions at n≥120 only (±4pp noise at n=40).
